## Active human reference: Soldier.glb

The character and clips are from Mixamo, as credited by the [official Three.js skeletal-animation example](https://threejs.org/examples/webgl_animation_skinning_blending.html). The downloaded file is [Soldier.glb in the Three.js example assets](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/blob/dev/examples/models/gltf/Soldier.glb).

This asset is governed by Adobe/Mixamo terms, not automatically by Three.js's software license. [Adobe's Mixamo FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html) describes permitted project uses. It is not labeled MIT, CC0, or original project artwork here.

Asset bytes, textures, skeleton and clips are unmodified. The viewer positions its display wrapper/camera and plays the supplied Walk by default. Idle, Run and TPose remain inspectable. This is a local learning reference, not the final game knight. Its checksum and structural information are in reports/reference-manifest.json.

Purpose: isolated animation-learning reference. It is not Examplerig.blend, the final game knight, or a newly authored animation. Structural information, checksum, and download address are recorded in reports/reference-manifest.json in the workbench.

Quaternius-UAL1-Standard.glb is a byte-identical copy of Unreal-Godot/UAL1_Standard.glb; in-place version. Source GLB unchanged; lower-body clips are derived in browser by filtering tracks.
